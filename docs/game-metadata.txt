  Serial       SCUS_974.79  (North America)
  Title        ATV Offroad Fury 4
  Developer    Climax Studios
  Publisher    Sony Computer Entertainment America
  Released     31 October 2006 (NA)
  Metacritic   77 / 100  (36 critics)
  Genre        Off-road racing

  The fourth and final entry in Sony's off-road series, and the first built by
  Climax rather than Rainbow Studios. It broadens the formula beyond quad bikes
  to dirt bikes, buggies and trophy trucks, each with distinct weight and
  suspension behaviour, across motocross circuits, desert, woodland and snow.
  The handling sits deliberately between arcade and simulation -- preloading the
  suspension before a jump matters -- and a generous trick system feeds a boost
  meter, so showing off is mechanically rewarded. Sixteen-player online was
  supported at launch, alongside four-player split screen.

  A substantial refinement rather than a reinvention. The campaign is
  restructured into kingdom-length Musou modes for Wei, Wu, Shu and an "Other"

  and randomised weapon attributes. It sold over a million copies in Japan within
  nine days.

  Developer    Square Enix
  Publisher    Square Enix
  Released     16 March 2006 (JP)

  The most structurally radical mainline Final Fantasy, developed under Yasumi
  Matsuno of Final Fantasy Tactics and set in his Ivalice, concerning a kingdom

  exceptional.

  Developer    Polyphony Digital
  Publisher    Sony Computer Entertainment America
  Released     9 July 2001 (NA)

  Genre        Racing simulation

  The PlayStation 2's technical showpiece and, for a long stretch, the
  best-selling game on the system. Polyphony rebuilt the physics and rendering

Scaler_US.iso
  Serial       SLUS_209.57  (North America)
  Title        Scaler
  Developer    Artificial Mind and Movement
  Publisher    Global Star Software
  Released     20 October 2004 (NA)
  Metacritic   69 / 100
  Genre        3D platformer

  An original, largely forgotten platformer about a twelve-year-old lizard
  enthusiast transformed into a reptilian creature and pulled into a parallel
  world. Its central mechanic is transformation: Scaler shifts between creature
  forms with different movement and combat abilities -- climbing, gliding,
  electrical attacks -- and levels are built around routing with the right form.
  The presentation is unusually strong for a budget release, with voice work
  including Andy Dick and Rider Strong. At 1.29 GB it is the smallest disc
  here.


ShadowOfTheColossus.iso
  Serial       SCPS_150.97  (Japan)
  Title        Shadow of the Colossus  /  Wanda to Kyozou (JP)
  Developer    Team Ico / SCE Japan Studio
  Publisher    Sony Computer Entertainment
  Released     18 October 2005 (NA)
  Metacritic   91 / 100
  Genre        Action adventure

  Fumito Ueda's follow-up to Ico, and one of the strongest arguments for games as
  an expressive medium produced in this era. A young man carries a dead girl to a
  forbidden land and is told she can be revived if he destroys sixteen colossi.
  There are no other enemies, no towns and almost no music outside the
  encounters -- the game alternates between long silent rides across an empty
  landscape and sixteen fights that are each simultaneously a battle and a
  climbing puzzle across a moving creature the size of a building. It withholds
  moral comfort deliberately.


SonicRiders_US.iso
  Serial       SLUS_213.31  (North America)
  Title        Sonic Riders
  Developer    Sonic Team
  Publisher    Sega
  Released     21 February 2006 (NA)
  Metacritic   55 / 100  (41 critics)
  Genre        Racing

  Sega's attempt to move Sonic into hoverboard racing, released for the series'
  fifteenth anniversary. Tracks have branching routes gated by character type --
  Speed grinds rails, Flight rides air currents, Power smashes obstacles -- so
  the same course runs differently depending on who is racing. The distinguishing
  mechanic is air as fuel: boosting, drifting and tricks all drain a gauge
  refilled by drafting or passing through rings, which reviewers found more
  punishing than interesting. Player reception has been considerably warmer than
  the critical response.


SoulCalibur2_JP.iso
  Serial       SLPS_252.30  (Japan)
  Title        SoulCalibur II
  Developer    Namco
  Publisher    Namco
  Released     27 August 2003 (NA)
  Metacritic   92 / 100  (41 critics)
  Genre        Fighting

  Widely regarded as the finest weapon-based fighting game ever made. Its
  eight-way run allows free movement around a three-dimensional arena rather than
  along a single plane, and the guard impact mechanic rewards precisely timed
  deflection over blocking, giving high-level play a distinctive rhythm. Each
  platform got an exclusive guest -- Heihachi on PS2, Link on GameCube, Spawn on
  Xbox. Weapon Master mode provides an extensive single-player campaign with a
  map, equipment and progression well beyond the arcade ladder.


StarWarsBattlefront2_US.iso
  Serial       SLUS_212.40  (North America)
  Title        Star Wars: Battlefront II
  Developer    Pandemic Studios
  Publisher    LucasArts
  Released     1 November 2005 (NA)
  Metacritic   78 / 100  (23 critics)
  Genre        First/third-person shooter

  The sequel that expanded Battlefront in nearly every direction. Space combat is
  the headline addition: full orbital battles where players fly starfighters,
  land inside enemy capital ships and sabotage them from within, all in one
  continuous match. Playable heroes -- Vader, Luke, Yoda -- can be earned
  mid-battle, and a narrative campaign follows the 501st Legion through both
  trilogies, including Order 66 from the clones' perspective. Its reputation
  among players sits well above its Metascore, which rests on only 23 reviews.


StarWarsBattlefront_US.iso
  Serial       SLUS_208.98  (North America)
  Title        Star Wars: Battlefront
  Developer    Pandemic Studios
  Publisher    LucasArts
  Released     20 September 2004 (NA)
  Metacritic   82 / 100  (36 critics)
  Genre        First/third-person shooter

  Battlefield's large-scale objective warfare transplanted into Star Wars, and
  the first game to let players fight the films' set-piece battles as ordinary
  infantry rather than a hero. Matches place dozens of combatants on maps from
  Hoth, Endor, Geonosis and Tatooine, contesting command posts that act as spawn
  points, with vehicles from speeder bikes to AT-ATs. Four classes per faction
  cover infantry, heavy weapons, sniping and engineering, and Galactic Conquest
  layers a light strategic campaign over the battles.


Suikoden4_JP.iso
  Serial       SLPM_656.00  (Japan)
  Title        Suikoden IV
  Developer    Konami Computer Entertainment Tokyo
  Publisher    Konami
  Released     19 August 2004 (JP)  |  11 January 2005 (NA)
  Metacritic   63 / 100
  Genre        Role-playing

  A prequel set roughly 150 years before the first Suikoden, in an island nation
  where the series' recruitment of 108 Stars of Destiny happens across an
  archipelago traversed by ship. The traditional elements are intact -- a large
  recruitable cast, a growing home base, army-scale battles and the rune system
  -- but the party drops from six to four and much of the play is sailing. That
  sailing is the source of most criticism: slow, frequent and interrupted by
  random encounters. The writing is better regarded than the aggregate
  suggests.


SuzukiTTSuperbikes_US.iso
  Serial       SLUS_209.12  (North America)
  Title        Suzuki TT Superbikes: Real Road Racing
  Developer    Jester Interactive
  Publisher    Valcon Games
  Released     2005
  Metacritic   71 / 100  (7 critics)
  Genre        Motorcycle racing simulation

  A licensed simulation of the Isle of Man TT, the public-road motorcycle race
  held on a 37.7-mile mountain circuit notoriously lethal and unlike anything in
  circuit racing. The game's ambition is the course itself, reproduced at full
  length and ridden against the clock in the race's staggered-start format rather
  than wheel-to-wheel. Handling is on the simulation side, with real stone walls
  and hedges punishing small errors immediately. Reviewers praised the fidelity
  while criticising loading times and the inevitable lack of track variety.


ThrillvilleOffTheRails_US.iso
  Serial       SLUS_216.11  (North America)
  Title        Thrillville: Off the Rails
  Developer    Frontier Developments
  Publisher    LucasArts
  Released     9 October 2007 (NA)
  Metacritic   70 / 100  (11 critics)
  Genre        Theme park management

  The sequel to Thrillville, developed by Frontier -- the studio behind
  RollerCoaster Tycoon 3 and later Planet Coaster -- as an accessible console take
  on park management. Players build and run five parks, designing coasters piece
  by piece and then riding them, managing staff and finances, and talking
  directly to guests. The "off the rails" addition is deliberately absurd
  physics: coasters can launch carriages between disconnected track sections and
  loop through impossible geometry. Around thirty mini-games sit inside the parks
  as attractions.


TonyHawkAW_US.iso
  Serial       SLUS_212.08  (North America)
  Title        Tony Hawk's American Wasteland
  Developer    Neversoft
  Publisher    Activision
  Released     18 October 2005 (NA)
  Metacritic   77 / 100  (44 critics)
  Genre        Sports / skateboarding

  The seventh Tony Hawk game and the series' attempt at a seamless open world:
  Los Angeles as one continuous map with loading disguised behind tunnels and
  alleys. The story follows a Midwestern arrival building a skate park in a
  derelict ranch, with art direction leaning on punk nostalgia. Mechanically it
  consolidates the accumulated moveset -- manuals, reverts, spine transfers, the
  wall plant, BMX carried over from Underground 2 -- and adds Bert slides.
  Reviewers split between calling it the most polished entry and feeling the
  annual formula had exhausted itself.
